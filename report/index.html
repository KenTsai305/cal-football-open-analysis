Offense: rebound, not a proven recovery

The latest FBS game produced more yards per play, but Cal’s rushing average remained below 3.0 at UNLV. Third-down conversion has stayed between 23% and 33% in every game after UCLA. Total points also overstate offensive output: Cal scored defensive touchdowns against Syracuse and Clemson. [G2] [G4] [G5]

Defense: better than the opener, still uneven

Raw run-defense results improved relative to UCLA; opponent and game-state differences remain. Yet the scoring records include UCLA rushing touchdowns of 31 and 53 yards in the fourth quarter, Clemson’s 75-yard rushing touchdown, and UNLV touchdowns on a 70-yard pass, 55-yard run and 36-yard pass. These examples show recurring major concessions, not a measured explosive-play rate or proof of their cause. [G1] [G4] [G5]

Organization: the latest game raises concern

Penalty counts were 6, 9, 8, 8 and 16. The latest spike is counterevidence to a claim that errors are steadily declining. Flags vary in meaning and officiating; a categorized, opportunity-based review is needed before calling this a coaching-discipline trend.

Observed gains and unresolved failures

Evidence of improvement

UNLV brought a one-game offensive rebound: 5.59 yards/play after 4.05 against Clemson. Opponent rushing averages have stayed below UCLA’s opening-game 8.7 yards/carry. These are observable gains, not proof of a sustained adjusted trend. [G1] [G4] [G5]

Evidence of regression and unresolved problems

Cal committed 16 penalties for 147 yards at UNLV. Its available opponent-adjusted offensive game ratings declined through Clemson, and major defensive concessions recur. No complete adjusted five-game trend has been verified. [G5] [A1]

Data-informed action: investigate and correct the documented unit and organizational failures, then evaluate whether those corrections work. The evidence supports intervention on specific problems now. It does not establish the superiority of retaining or replacing the HC, OC or DC; neither action receives a default presumption here.

Special teams also show mixed evidence: available adjusted ratings improve against Syracuse and fall against Clemson. The Clemson punt-return touchdown and UNLV net-punting rebound belong in the assessment alongside offense and defense. [A4] Special-teams diagnostics.

Methodology, limitations and independent fact-check

How to interpret these results

This is a provisional snapshot of limited early-season results and reported available budgets, not audited paid spending. Open CFB is an independent results-and-margin model, not ESPN SOR, FPI or per-play efficiency. It adjusts scores for opponents and location, with capped margins, a win bonus, recency weighting and regularization. No preseason team expectations or inherited-condition adjustments are used. Current ESPN SOR/efficiency tables could not be verified, so this model is used as an explicit alternative. [3] [4]

One model cannot establish the causal effect of spending or staff experience. Budget ranges overlap and are uncertain. The finding is the observed position within each defined group, not a financial ROI percentage. Full current-season spending is not established in this research.

What belongs in an annual-investment total

Use 2026 recurring coaching and football-support compensation, other recurring football expenses, and program-associated player payments. Count each expense once. School-paid athlete compensation may already be in operating expenses; outside collective payments may not be.

School revenue-sharing payments and program-associated NIL are roster investment; media distributions to the school are revenue. Independent player endorsements are excluded here. No separate revenue-share or NIL figure has been substantiated consistently across these schools, so the combined budget estimate is shown without an invented split. [2] [22]

Buyouts and facilities spending belong in separate lines. Historical FY2024/FY2024–25 expenses have been removed from the comparison: they do not establish current-season investment. No profitability requirement, inherited roster quality, or prior-season outcome adjustment is applied.

What would strengthen the finding

Verified current payments and full payroll; a second independently verified results metric; season-end results; and sensitivity checks using alternative budget bands and range endpoints. Staff experience should remain visible as a management choice, rather than normalize away the hiring risk.

What can—and cannot—measure coaching improvement

No validated high-correlation score for this staff’s coaching quality was established in this review. Opponent-adjusted unit ratings measure team output; organizational errors and recurring breakdowns are coach-sensitive diagnostics. Neither separates the coach from players, opponents, injuries or random variation.
